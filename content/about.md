## Welcome

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at School of Geosciences, University of Edinburgh, working with [Professor Andrew Curtis](https://blogs.ed.ac.uk/curtis/). I develop probabilistic methods for imaging and understanding the Earth's subsurface, with a particular interest in how uncertainty can become a source of scientific insight rather than a limitation to be ignored.

My research sits at the intersection of geophysical inverse theory, seismic imaging and computational statistics. I work on efficient Bayesian and variational inference approaches that make it possible to estimate not only a plausible subsurface model, but also the family of models that fits the data. This perspective is useful wherever decisions depend on incomplete observations: from seismic tomography and full waveform inversion to monitoring carbon storage and characterising groundwater systems.

## Research interests

My current interests include:

- **Bayesian geophysical imaging and inversion** - scalable inference methods for seismic tomography, full waveform inversion and time-lapse monitoring;
- **Uncertainty quantification** - using posterior uncertainty to evaluate geological hypotheses, recognise interpretation bias and support risk-aware decisions;
- **Seismic wavefield modelling and migration** - accurate and efficient algorithms for attenuative media and large-scale 3D problems;
- **Geophysical applications for the energy transition** - especially subsurface characterisation and monitoring for CO2 storage.

I am also developing [GeoPVI](https://github.com/XuebinZhaoZXB/GeoPVI), an open-source Python framework for geoscientific inversion using parametric variational inference methods.

## Background

I received my PhD in Geophysics from the University of Edinburgh, where I was supervised by [Professor Andrew Curtis](https://blogs.ed.ac.uk/curtis/). My doctoral work investigated practical advances in Bayesian inference for seismic imaging and interrogation problems. Before moving to Edinburgh, I completed MSc and BSc degrees at China University of Petroleum (Beijing).

Alongside research, I contribute to teaching and academic service at the University of Edinburgh. I have supported courses in geophysical imaging, inversion and near-surface geophysics, supervise student research projects, and organise the University's Geophysics weekly seminar.
